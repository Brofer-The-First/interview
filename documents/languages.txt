Hebrew is my mother tongue, and I absolutely love it. Apart from feeling most comfortable in it, I enjoy its rich morphology and potential to create new words. I also enjoy the fact that modern Hebrew is a mixture of different historical layers — Biblical, Mishnaic, Medieval — along with foreign influences, as its founding speakers came from various countries.

I reached proficiency in English during high school, mostly through gaming and English pop culture, but also through the Israeli education system — I usually had good English teachers.

I learned Arabic (Levantine dialect) during my military service. I continued practicing it in various opportunities afterwards, but eventually set it aside somewhat in favor of German.

After learning Arabic in the army, I decided to learn more languages and picked up German. I started with Duolingo but quickly moved on to seeking out German speakers in Tel Aviv, as well as learning through songs, podcasts, and TV shows. I met my fiancée through a German-Hebrew language exchange. Later, I moved to Germany for her studies, where I worked and became proficient in the language.

After feeling comfortable with German, I moved on to French. I'd like to invest more time in it by actively speaking with native speakers, but I'm currently sticking to daily practice on Duolingo. I'd estimate my level at around B1–B2.

I can understand most Yiddish thanks to my knowledge of German and Hebrew. I took a few courses at the Oxford School of Rare Jewish Languages and would like to improve further, but have other priorities at the moment. I also read Aramaic texts — Talmud, the Book of Daniel, prayers and carols — and learn as much as possible on the go, though I've never studied it formally. Both Yiddish and Aramaic draw me because of my interest in Judaism.
